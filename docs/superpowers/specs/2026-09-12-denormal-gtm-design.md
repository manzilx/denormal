# Denormal Labs — go-to-market design

**Date:** 12 September 2026
**Status:** approved in brainstorming; not yet executed
**Scope:** Denormal Labs as a firm (the five featured systems), not a single product

---

## 1. The binding constraints

These are not preferences. Every decision below is downstream of them.

| Constraint | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Single director, employed full-time elsewhere | No sales motion that needs daytime availability or travel |
| Discretion is the hard requirement | No public byline, no founder profile, no speaking, no indexable trail |
| Zero sales, zero references, zero logos | Every conventional trust signal is unavailable |
| No certifications, no SSO, one maintainer | Cannot survive a procurement-led enterprise sale |
| Ten systems built, five featured | Breadth is a credibility liability at the door, an asset once trust exists |

**Goal, twelve months:** two or three paying customers, and optionality — advisory work, partnership, or acquisition interest — without leaving the job. Explicitly *not* replacing the salary, and explicitly *not* free design partners: money on the table is the validation being sought.

### The constraint that had to be corrected

"Never named" is achievable for marketing and impossible at contract. In India a Pvt Ltd or LLP puts the director's name and DIN on publicly searchable MCA master data; GST registration exposes the legal name; a sole proprietorship puts it directly on the invoice. Money surfaces the name.

The real constraint is therefore **no public byline and no indexable trail** — not "no one ever knows". A buyer under NDA knowing who they are paying is not a breach. A LinkedIn post is. The whole plan is built on that line.

## 2. Trust architecture

An anonymous vendor with no customers asking for money is, structurally, the scam pattern. Most plans in this position cheat: a stock-photo team, "20 years' experience" attached to nobody, invented customer logos. All of it reads as fraud precisely *because* anonymous plus generic is what fraud looks like.

The way out is the inverse. **Anonymous plus unfakeable specificity reads as credible.** A bio can be faked. Knowing that an extension-of-time claim dies on concurrency, that near-miss counts are under-reported until leading indicators become fiction, or that three requirement sources contradict and nothing records why one won — that cannot. A reader who knows the work recognises the writer in two paragraphs, and that recognition does the job a founder bio would do. Critically, it survives being forwarded to someone who will never meet him.

Three mechanisms, all already partly in place:

1. **Demonstrated tacit knowledge.** Everything published must carry detail only a practitioner has. This is the strategy, not a content tactic.
2. **Claims that survive checking.** Already enforced on the site, and maintained — Peak Logic's reference schedule now states it is generated rather than client data, and Sentinel's corpus count was corrected from three authorities to four against `corpus_manifest.json`. An anonymous vendor whose numbers all check out is more trustworthy than a named one whose numbers do not.
3. **Volunteered limits.** The "Where the line is" panel — no SOC 2, no ISO 27001, no encryption at rest, no SSO, self-asserted identity headers on several systems — is the single strongest available trust signal, because nothing dishonest ever does it.

## 3. Advisors

**Position: yes, but not yet, and not as advisors.**

Recruiting a named advisor today costs discretion with someone inside the professional circle being hidden from, and buys a name on a website backed by no evidence. The best veterans decline to lend credibility to something unproven, so the ask also spends the relationship that would matter later.

What veterans are needed for now is **correction, not endorsement**: does this match how the work actually runs?

- **Now — design reviewers.** Three to five. Private, unpaid, no equity, no public listing. Told what is being built, not how far it is intended to go.
- **At first revenue — named advisors.** One or two, public, small equity or fee. By then a pilot exists, the ask is credible, and the discretion calculus has changed anyway.

Equity today is also poor mechanics: granting it before incorporation is meaningless and messy to re-grant.

## 4. The spine — publish the instruments, not the opinions

Industry people do not forward opinions. They forward things they can use. Every product already contains a proprietary instrument that is useful standalone:

| Product | Instrument to publish |
|---|---|
| Peak Logic | The 12 schedule-quality issue codes and A–F scoring — "grade your own schedule" |
| Power Contract Intelligence | The 13 fixed questions every tender pack is read against |
| OneLegal | 20 of the ~300 playbook questions, by contract family |
| Sentinel | A near-miss reporting-health self-check |
| Quality AI | The precedence worksheet: three sources, one governing requirement |

Each is unfakeably expert, immediately useful, requires no author, and closes on the same line: *this is one of twelve checks our engine runs.* A diagnostic also returns a result the reader wants to discuss, which manufactures the conversation opener that is otherwise unavailable.

**Cadence: one substantial artifact every six to eight weeks.** Five to eight in a year is the honest ceiling for an employed solo operator, and low volume suits a brand whose argument is rigour.

**Distribution — the weak point, stated plainly.** "Brand only" leaves almost no way to move these. Posting them attaches his name to the act even when the document carries no byline. So: **he authors; he does not distribute.** Documents go to design reviewers and a small number of trusted contacts, who forward. The site is the one self-serve channel — search-indexed, no person required, slow, compounding.

## 5. The engine — the reviewers are the pipeline

Do not run two motions. The three-to-five design reviewers and the fifteen-to-twenty named target accounts are the same population, approached with the same ask.

The ask is never "buy this". It is **"I'd value thirty minutes of your read on this."** That framing is true, flatters correctly, is hard to refuse rudely, and qualifies the person in the same motion — someone who engages seriously with a schedule-quality checklist has the problem.

**Path:** artifact → private send → thirty-minute call about *their* work, not the product → if the problem is real, offer a scoped diagnosis → the diagnosis becomes the pilot proposal.

The diagnosis is the hinge. It is the first chargeable thing, and it converts a stranger into someone who has already paid once.

**Honest funnel, twelve months:** 20 contacts → 8 reply → 4 real conversations → 2 diagnoses → 1–2 paid pilots.

That is **three to four paying relationships**, counting diagnosis buyers as customers — which they are, and which is how the twelve-month goal should be measured. Anything better is luck, not plan.

**Tracking:** one sheet. Who received what, and what came back. Not a CRM.

## 6. Pricing

Software pricing is unavailable: no certifications, no support organisation, no references, one maintainer. A per-seat licence invites exactly the procurement scrutiny that would be lost. Price against **the cost of the problem**, and structure each step so the buyer's risk shrinks before his does.

**Step 1 — paid diagnosis. ₹75,000–₹1,50,000, two weeks.**
They supply a real artifact: a P6 schedule, a tender pack, six months of near-miss reports, a stack of NCRs. A written findings document comes back. This is the product demonstrating itself on their data, delivered alone, with no software risk and no deployment. Sold as professional services under a scope of work — no licence, no MSA. Paid in full before the findings are delivered, which is normal for a fixed-scope engagement of this size and removes the only way this step can fail. The number is chosen against the threshold a department head can approve without raising a tender, not against value delivered.

**Step 2 — scoped pilot. ₹3,00,000–₹8,00,000, ten to twelve weeks.**
One workflow, one site or project, written completion criteria, 50% up front. Anchor on what the problem costs: a forensic delay analysis from a claims consultant runs ₹15–40 lakh, so grading three schedules and producing a defensible EOT position at ₹5 lakh is self-evidently cheap. That comparison is the pricing argument; a feature list is not.

**Fixed fee, never a day rate.** A day rate discloses that the supplier is one person. A fixed fee sells an outcome and keeps capacity private.

**Step 3 — annual licence, only after a pilot proves out. ₹6,00,000–₹15,00,000.**
Priced **per project or per site**, never per seat — it matches how EPC and manufacturing budgets are drawn, and stops revenue scaling with headcount that cannot be supported. Cap the number sold deliberately: five annual contracts is a support catastrophe for one person, and failing them costs more than the revenue.

**Four prohibitions.** No public price list — it commoditises and invites comparison with funded competitors. No free trial. No per-seat anything. **No early-customer discount** — the first price set becomes the permanent ceiling.

Instead of discounting, give **scope**: the first two customers get a permanent price lock and real roadmap influence. Free today, genuinely valuable to them, headline number intact.

Gulf buyers: quote in USD at roughly two to two and a half times the India figure.

## 7. Sequence

**Q1 — make the channels work, assemble the list.**
Close the two live gaps first: Cloudflare Email Routing for `hello@denormal.in`, and Resend verification plus `RESEND_API_KEY` so the intake form delivers. Marketing a site whose two contact routes both fail is the cheapest possible own goal. Recruit three to five design reviewers. Build the list of twenty named accounts. Publish artifact #1 — the Peak Logic schedule-quality checklist, which is the most self-contained and the easiest to act on alone.

**Q2 — first sends, first conversations.**
Artifact #2 (the 13 tender questions). First ten private sends. Target two real conversations — half the year's total, since the second ten sends benefit from what the first ten teach. Attempt the first paid diagnosis.

**Q3 — first money, first evidence.**
Artifact #3 (OneLegal playbook extract). One or two diagnoses delivered. Convert one to a pilot. Reassess which of the five is actually pulling — the answer will not be the one expected.

**Q4 — consolidate.**
Artifact #4. One or two pilots running. Convert one or two reviewers into named advisors, now that evidence exists. Decide whether to open the partner-led channel in year two.

## 8. Failure modes

| Failure | Early signal | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Artifacts written, nobody forwards them | Fewer than three replies from the first ten sends | The list is wrong, not the artifact. Rebuild the list before writing artifact #3 |
| A reviewer talks, discretion breaks | Any mention reaching a colleague | Assume anything told to one person is public. Brief reviewers explicitly at recruitment |
| Diagnosis becomes free consulting | Scope creep, no invoice | Written scope, fixed fee, defined deliverable, payment before delivery of findings |
| Attention spread across five products | Conversations that never reach a second call | Lead with one product per conversation. The portfolio is context, not a menu |
| Selling more than one person can carry | A second pilot before the first is stable | The deliberate cap. Decline, or delay start dates |
| Employment conflict surfaces | A prospect who shares a customer, supplier or director with the employer | Read the employment contract before the first invoice. Disqualify any account with that overlap, however good the fit |

**What would falsify this plan:** if after ten sends and four real conversations nobody will pay ₹75,000 for a diagnosis, the problem is not marketing. It is that the software does not address a *budgeted* problem. That is the honest test, and it arrives cheaply — which is the main argument for this sequence.

---

## 9. Deliberately excluded

- **Paid advertising** — no landing-page conversion path, no attribution, and spend is visible.
- **SEO content volume** — dilutes the one asset that works, which is density of expertise.
- **Conferences and speaking** — the discretion constraint forbids it.
- **Cold email at scale** — destroys an anonymous brand's only asset, which is seeming considered.
- **Free tier or product-led growth** — needs self-serve onboarding, support, and a security posture that does not exist.
- **Partner-led distribution** — genuinely the fastest route to revenue, and held for year two. It spends discretion first: a channel partner in Indian power EPC very likely knows the employer.
