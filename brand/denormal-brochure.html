Deviation register → go/no-go

What it is

A workspace for reading a tender pack before you bid or before you award. The pack goes in; risks, deviations, pre-bid queries and a clause map come out.

What it does

Reads hundreds of pages of general and special conditions, scope, bill of quantities and tax appendices against the same thirteen questions every time — penalties, warranty, local content, change in law, termination — then carries the findings through three review gates to a recorded go/no-go.

Why buy it

What sinks a contract is the clause nobody had time to read. Bid decisions are made in days; the pack is read by whoever was free. This makes the reading uniform, so coverage does not depend on who ran it or how busy that week was.

How it helps you

Your committee receives a deviation register, a risk register, pre-bid queries and the gate records as one workbook. Every finding cites the clause it came from, so a reviewer can check it rather than take it on trust.

What you get back

Man-hours

One pack, read once, against the same thirteen questions — rather than each reviewer reading as far as the calendar allowed. Your arithmetic: staff-days per tender pack today × packs per year.

Records digitised

The deviation register, pre-bid queries, pricing assumptions and all three gate decisions stop being an email thread and become one workbook, every finding cited to the clause it came from.

Money

The exposure you avoid is the clause nobody had time to read. Price it the way your own contracts do — the LD ladder, the change-in-law position, the local-content undertaking you would otherwise have accepted unpriced.

What it is

A workbench for contract review and dispute work. A contract set is read into an isolated matter and returned as a risk matrix with clause-level citations.

What it does

Maps a contract against roughly three hundred canonical questions across eleven contract families, including FIDIC, IChemE, Orgalime and the NTPC general conditions. For a dispute, counsel curates an evidence set and seals it; drafting may then run only over that frozen set.

Why buy it

A six-hundred-page contract is unreadable under deal pressure. And in a dispute, drafting over a corpus that keeps growing means you can never state precisely what a submission was based on — which is the first question the other side asks.

How it helps you

Deviation review that occupies an officer for a fortnight becomes a day with a written trail. The sealed set means that when you say what a claim was built from, you can prove it — to the tribunal, and to audit.

What you get back

Man-hours

Deviation review against your general conditions, on every bidder, without the review shortening as the deadline approaches. Your arithmetic: reviewer-days per bidder × bidders per tender.

Records digitised

A contract set becomes a searchable matter with clause-level citation, and an evidence set can be sealed so a draft states exactly what it was built from — instead of a corpus that has moved on since.

Money

External counsel hours on first-pass review, and the far larger figure behind them: claims that fail on documentation rather than entitlement. Your own arbitration history is the honest input here.

What it is

A critical-path scheduling and forensic delay-analysis platform. It reads a Primavera P6 schedule, grades it, then produces a defensible extension-of-time position.

What it does

Imports the XER directly, runs its own CPM engine over it — forward and backward pass, float, calendars, constraints, lag — then applies delay events one at a time in an SCL Protocol time-impact analysis with Malmaison attribution.

Why buy it

Building an extension-of-time claim takes weeks, and the number has to survive an opposing expert who will attack the method before the arithmetic. Meanwhile you need a Primavera licence simply to open the file.

How it helps you

You get a cumulative waterfall showing how the number was produced, not just the number. A schedule-quality gate blocks the analysis until the programme is fit to analyse, and any override is recorded.

What you get back

Man-hours

A schedule graded and a delay analysis run event by event, in place of weeks of manual forensic work. Your arithmetic: analyst-weeks per claim × claims in dispute.

Records digitised

Baselines, schedule-quality scores, delay events and the attribution behind the entitlement become a stored, re-runnable record rather than one consultant’s working file.

Money

A forensic delay analysis bought from a claims consultant is a market rate you can check against quotes you already hold. Add the Primavera licences currently needed simply to open the file.

What it is

A mobile-first environment, health and safety platform for plants and construction sites. A worker photographs a hazard; a cited countermeasure comes back.

What it does

Classifies the photograph against a twenty-one-type hazard taxonomy, drafts the report, and retrieves countermeasures from a corpus spanning OSHA, HSE UK, NIOSH and Lean material — each answer carrying its citation chain and a grounding score.

Why buy it

Safety software collects reports. The gap is the loop from an observation to a corrected condition. Near-misses are under-reported to the point where leading indicators stop meaning anything, because reporting one costs twelve minutes of paperwork.

How it helps you

Reporting takes under a minute from the phone already in the worker’s hand, which is the only intervention that reliably moves reporting rates. Permit-to-work audits return PASS, FAIL or UNCERTAIN against a cited standard rather than one inspector’s opinion.

What you get back

Man-hours

Reporting a hazard takes under a minute from the phone already in the worker’s hand. Your arithmetic: minutes per paper report today × reports per site per month × sites.

Records digitised

Near-miss and hazard data becomes real rather than notional, with photograph, location, capture-time and the cited standard attached — a contemporaneous record, not one reconstructed after an incident.

Money

This is the product where labour saved is the smallest part. The number that matters is one lost-time injury: enquiry, stoppage, statutory exposure, and the ministry correspondence that follows.

Inspection and non-conformance

→

One governing requirement → ITP

What it is

Document-grounded quality control for manufacturing and project work packages. Requirements in, inspection and test plan out, non-conformance closed with evidence.

What it does

Breaks specifications into individually testable requirements, each returned with its clause and exact character span, then applies a deterministic precedence rule to decide which of the conflicting sources governs.

Why buy it

Three requirement sources stack on every activity — the client specification, the corporate standard, the applicable code — and they contradict one another. Today an engineer reconciles them by hand, under time pressure, and nothing records why one won.

How it helps you

The governing requirement is recorded with its reasoning. Inspection hold points are enforced rather than advisory: skip one and the system refuses. The audit log is append-only and written in the same transaction as the change it records.

What you get back

Man-hours

Three specifications reconciled into one governing requirement with the reasoning recorded, instead of an engineer doing it by hand per activity. Your arithmetic: hours per work package × packages per project.

Records digitised

Requirements, the precedence decision, the inspection and test plan and every non-conformance become a linked record with an append-only audit trail written in the same transaction as the change.

Money

Rework and the cost of poor quality, plus the schedule cost of a hold point discovered late. Your NCR register already holds both figures.

Data security

Where your data is stored.

Every one of these systems is a self-contained service. It does not need to live with us, and in almost every case it should not. These are the three ways it can be deployed, in the order we would recommend them.

Recommended

On your own servers

It runs inside your perimeter, on hardware you already own and already audit. Your existing access control, backup and retention policies apply unchanged. Nothing of yours is in our hands, so there is nothing for us to lose, disclose, or hand back when the contract ends.

Also good

In your own cloud account

If you already hold a contract with a MeitY-empanelled cloud provider — AWS Mumbai and Azure India among them — we deploy into your tenancy. You inherit your own empanelment, your own audit trail and your own controls, and procurement has nothing new to assess. For a PSU with an existing cloud footprint this is usually the shortest route.

Only if you prefer

Hosted by us

We run it for you in an India region, so the data stays in the country. We would still rather you chose one of the options above: they keep the data inside your control and under your compliance posture rather than ours, which is a better answer for you and an easier one for your auditors.

What is held, and what is not

  • Held: the documents you supply — the tender pack, the contract set, the schedule, the photographs.
  • Held: the record derived from them — findings, citations, decisions, and the audit trail that shows how each was reached.
  • Not held: anything else. No copies, no analytics on your content, no shadow index.
  • Not done: we do not train on your material. There is no training pipeline in any of these systems — not a policy we have chosen to follow, but a component that does not exist.

Some features can call an external model service. Before you deploy, we will tell you exactly which product calls which service, and precisely what that service receives — in writing, so your security team can assess it rather than take our word for it. Where you would rather nothing left your network at all, say so and we will scope the deployment that way.

What is not built yetRoadmap, not shipped
  • Hosted deployment on MeitY-empanelled infrastructureToday our own hosting is an India region but not an empanelled offering. If you want us to host, this is the gap to close first — and deploying into your own tenancy sidesteps it entirely.
  • Encryption at rest, applied by the applicationOn your own infrastructure, the disk and volume encryption you already run covers this. As a hosted service, it is work we still owe you.
  • Single sign-on against your directoryToday these are built for a trusted network behind your own perimeter authentication, not for the open internet.
  • A formal retention, deletion and legal-hold policyDeployed with you, retention follows your policy on your storage. As a product feature, it is not there yet.

We would rather write that list down than have you find it during a security review. Every claim in this brochure is meant to survive being checked — which only means anything if we are equally plain about what is missing.
